Why choosing the right curtains matters more than you think.

Curtains are one of the few things in a home that touch light, privacy, sound, temperature and mood all at once. Get them right and a room feels finished without trying. Get them wrong and no amount of furniture rearranging quite fixes it.

Why the right curtains matter more than you think

It's easy to treat curtains as a finishing touch — something you pick last, once the furniture and paint are sorted. In practice, they do more work than almost anything else in the room. They control how much heat and glare comes through a west-facing window in the afternoon. They decide whether a bedroom actually gets dark enough to sleep in. They soften hard surfaces and absorb sound in a way paint and furniture can't.

The cost of getting it wrong isn't just aesthetic. Curtains that are too thin don't block Mysore's afternoon sun. Fabric that isn't colourfast fades within a year. A length that's slightly off makes an otherwise well-designed room look unfinished. And because curtains are made to measure, undoing a mistake usually means starting over — not a quick exchange. (See our cost guide for a sense of what's actually involved.)

Start with what the room actually needs

Before thinking about fabric or colour, be honest about what the room is for. A living room usually needs flexibility: light during the day, privacy in the evening. A bedroom often needs genuine darkness and quiet more than it needs a view. A kitchen or dining space usually just needs something easy to keep clean.

  • Living rooms: layering a sheer with a heavier curtain gives you control through the day.
  • Bedrooms: prioritise blackout or heavier linings over pure style if sleep quality matters to you.
  • Kitchens and dining areas: lighter, washable fabrics hold up better than delicate ones.

Choose fabric with Mysore's climate in mind

Fabric choice matters more here than in a cooler climate. Heavier fabrics like velvet look rich but can trap heat in a room that already gets warm through the day. Sheers and linens breathe better and suit most living spaces, but won't block much light or heat on their own — which is why layering is so common in practice.

Sun exposure is the other factor people underestimate. A window that gets direct afternoon sun will fade an untreated fabric within a year or two. Look for fade-resistant weaves or linings on any window with strong western exposure, even if it costs a little more upfront.

Get the length, fullness and mounting right

Two curtains in identical fabric can look completely different depending on length and fullness. Curtains that just clear the floor read as tidy and modern; a slight break or puddle at the floor reads as more traditional and formal. There's no universally "correct" choice — it should match the rest of the room.

Fullness (how much fabric is used relative to the width of the window) is what makes curtains look tailored rather than flat. Skimping on fabric to save cost is one of the most common reasons made-to-measure curtains end up looking underwhelming despite good fabric and colour choices.

Colour and pattern: fit the room, not the trend

It's tempting to choose curtains the way you'd choose a piece of clothing — based on what looks good on its own. But curtains cover a large surface area and interact with everything else in the room: the wall colour, the flooring, the furniture upholstery. A pattern that looks striking on a fabric swatch can overwhelm a room at full scale, and a colour that looks neutral in a shop can shift completely under your home's actual lighting.

This is one of the main reasons seeing fabric in your own room, in your own light, matters more than choosing from a catalogue or a screen.

Why professional measuring changes everything

Most of the curtain mistakes we're asked to fix later come down to measuring — a rod mounted too low, a width that doesn't account for fullness, a length measured without accounting for the flooring underneath. These are easy mistakes to make once, and expensive ones to redo.

This is really the whole reason we built The Draper around home visits rather than a showroom. Seeing your actual windows, your actual light and your actual furniture removes almost all the guesswork — and it's a big part of why made-to-measure exists in the first place.
