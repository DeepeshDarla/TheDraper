Curtains vs blinds: which is right for your Mysore home?

There's no universal winner between curtains and blinds — only a better fit for a specific room, a specific window and how you actually live in it. Here's how to think it through properly, room by room.

Light control: blinds win precision, curtains win range

Blinds give you fine control — tilt the slats a few degrees and you can filter light without losing the view entirely. That precision is hard to beat for a home office or a kitchen window above a sink.

Curtains work more in extremes: fully open for maximum light, fully closed for maximum darkness. Layering a sheer with a heavier curtain gets you a middle ground, but it's still less precise, minute-to-minute, than a blind.

Privacy: both work, but differently

Blinds give consistent, adjustable privacy at any time of day without affecting how much light gets in. Curtains give more complete privacy when closed, but the room usually goes darker at the same time — there's no in-between position the way there is with slats.

For a street-facing living room where you want privacy in the evening without sitting in the dark, blinds (or a sheer curtain layer) usually work better than heavy curtains alone.

Maintenance and durability in Mysore's climate

This is where the decision gets practical fast. Fabric blinds and curtains both collect dust, but curtains have far more surface area and folds for it to settle into — they typically need washing more often. Wooden and PVC blinds wipe clean in minutes, which matters more than people expect once a home is a few years old.

Heat and sun exposure matter too. On a window with strong western sun, both fabric curtains and fabric blinds will fade over time unless treated; wooden or aluminium blinds hold up to direct sun better than most fabrics.

Style: curtains soften a room, blinds sharpen it

Curtains add softness, movement and a sense of scale — floor-to-ceiling curtains in particular make a room feel taller and more finished. Blinds read as cleaner and more architectural, and tend to suit smaller windows, home offices and more minimal interiors better than heavy drapery would.

Neither is more "modern" than the other by default — it depends on the rest of the room's materials and furniture.

Cost: blinds are usually the lower entry point

As a general rule, made-to-measure blinds cost less than made-to-measure curtains of comparable quality, largely because they use less material. But this varies a lot by type and fabric — a premium wooden or motorised blind can easily cost more than a simple curtain. Get an actual quote for your specific windows rather than assuming based on category alone.

You don't have to choose only one

Many of the best-looking rooms we work on use both: a functional blind close to the glass for daily light and privacy control, with curtains layered in front for softness, framing and full blackout when needed. It's a common enough combination in Mysore homes that we built a dedicated guide for it — see curtains and blinds together if that's the direction you're leaning.

A quick way to decide

  • Home office or study: blinds, for precise glare control during work hours.
  • Bedroom: curtains (ideally with blackout lining), or curtains layered over a blind if you want both darkness and daytime control — see our bedroom window coverings guide for more.
  • Living room: either works well; curtains if you want softness and scale, blinds if the room is more minimal or the window is smaller.
  • Kitchen: blinds, for easier cleaning and better heat/moisture tolerance.
  • Street-facing rooms: blinds or a sheer layer, for privacy without losing all the light.
