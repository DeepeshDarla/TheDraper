Best window coverings for bedrooms.

A bedroom has one job a living room doesn't: helping you sleep. That changes almost every decision, from fabric weight to how the curtains actually close.

Start from sleep, not style

It's easy to choose bedroom curtains the same way you'd choose living room curtains — by colour and fabric first. But a bedroom succeeds or fails on darkness, quiet and how it feels first thing in the morning. Style still matters, but it should come after you've settled how dark and how private the room needs to be. Once you know that, our guide on choosing the right curtains covers fabric, length and style in more depth.

Blackout is about the lining, not the fabric thickness

A common misconception is that a heavier or darker-coloured curtain will block light. In practice, light control comes almost entirely from the lining, not the face fabric. A proper blackout lining behind a light, pretty fabric will out-perform a heavy fabric with no lining at all — which means you don't have to sacrifice style for darkness.

If you're a light sleeper, or the room faces east and gets early morning sun, this is the one detail worth prioritising over almost everything else.

Privacy without losing airflow

Bedrooms in Mysore often need to stay private with windows open for ventilation, especially outside the hottest months. A sheer curtain layered behind a heavier one gives you privacy and airflow together — closing just the sheer during the day, and adding the heavier layer (or a blackout blind) at night.

For street-facing or close-set-apartment bedrooms, this layering is usually a better answer than one heavy curtain used all the time, which tends to make the room feel shut in during the day.

Noise and temperature: a secondary but real benefit

Curtains won't soundproof a room, but a fuller, heavier curtain does noticeably dampen street noise and traffic compared with a blind or a thin fabric — useful for a bedroom on a busier road. The same fuller fabric also helps even out a room's temperature, holding in cool air overnight and blocking radiant heat from a sun-facing window during the day.

Safety, especially for children's rooms

Corded blinds are a genuine safety hazard in any room a young child sleeps or plays in. If you're choosing blinds for a kid's bedroom, go cordless or motorised rather than a traditional pull-cord design — it costs a little more but removes the risk entirely. Motorised curtains and blinds are worth considering here even if you wouldn't choose motorisation elsewhere in the house; see our motorisation options if that's a priority for a nursery or kids' room.

Putting it together

  • Light sleeper or east-facing window: prioritise a proper blackout lining over fabric choice.
  • Street-facing or overlooked windows: layer a sheer with a heavier curtain or blind for privacy without losing airflow.
  • Busy road or shared wall: a fuller, heavier curtain helps more than a blind with noise and temperature.
  • Children's or nursery rooms: choose cordless or motorised blinds, never a traditional pull cord.
